# PORTFOLYO — VISUAL DNA

Abstracted from the benchmark. Applied to all outputs.

## THE PORTFOLIO

### First Contact

The screen is almost entirely black. Not dark gray. Black. #0a0a0a.

In the top-right corner, a crimson orb breathes—massive, blurred, barely there. It doesn't demand attention. It exists at the edge of perception, like heat in a darkroom.

Then the name lands.

Two words. Massive. Bebas Neue at 7rem or larger. The first name solid white. The last name outlined—just a single pixel stroke of crimson against nothing. This is not a greeting. It is a statement of territory.

Below the name, smaller but unhurried: the title. One line. Space Grotesk. No explanation, no tagline, no "passionate about" anything. Just what you are.

The scroll indicator doesn't bounce. It pulses once, slowly, then stops. An invitation, not a command.

There is nothing else on the first screen. No navigation bar crowding the moment. No "let's connect" button begging for attention. Just name, title, and darkness.

The person looking at this knows immediately: whoever made this is not trying to impress them.

### The Seeking Banner

If seeking, it appears below the fold. Not above—that would be desperate.

A subtle container. Not a card with shadows and borders. Just a hint of crimson at 5% opacity, a hairline border at 20%. Inside:

- A pulsing dot. Emerald if actively seeking, amber if considering.
- Three words: "Söker LIA-plats" or "Open to opportunities"
- Three data points, arranged horizontally: Period. Location. Interests.

Each data point has a micro-label in uppercase, letter-spaced, 0.75rem, zinc-500. The values below in white, 0.875rem.

This banner communicates: I am available. These are my terms. That is all.

No "I would love to hear from you!" No exclamation marks anywhere on the page.

### About

The section title: "OM MIG" in Bebas Neue, 4rem, left-aligned. No decorative line underneath. No icon beside it.

Then the body. 700px max-width. Not centered—left-aligned, as if reading a book. Space Grotesk at 1.125rem, line-height 1.8. The color is not white but zinc-400—enough contrast to read, not enough to shout.

Two paragraphs maximum. Each paragraph does work. The first establishes context. The second establishes capability. No third paragraph for personality. Personality is in the choices already made.

If there are highlights (years of experience, products shipped, teams led), they appear as a horizontal row of four items. Each item: the number in 1.5rem bold, the label in micro caps below. No icons. No progress bars. Just facts.

### Timeline

This section uses vertical space like breath.

A single 1px line runs down the left edge—zinc-800, nearly invisible. Experience entries attach to it with small crimson dots.

Each entry:

- Period on top, micro caps, zinc-500
- Title in white, 1.25rem, semibold
- Company/institution in zinc-400
- Description optional, zinc-400, 0.9375rem

The most recent entry has a subtle glow around its dot—box-shadow at 4px, accent at 30% opacity. This says "current" without a label.

Entries are spaced 2.5rem apart. This is not tight. Tight suggests you need to fit more in. This says: each thing I did deserves its own air.

### Projects

Two-column grid on desktop. Not three—that would feel like a catalog.

Each project card:

- Background: zinc-900 (the card level, not the page level)
- Padding: 1.5rem
- Border-radius: 1rem
- On hover: lifts 4px, shadow appears beneath like the card is lifting off the page

Inside:

- Project name first. 1.25rem, white, semibold.
- One sentence description. Not what it does—what it is. Not features—essence.
- Tech stack as small pills below. zinc-700 background, zinc-400 text, 0.75rem.

If there's an image, it's above the text. Not a screenshot—that looks amateur. Either a polished mockup with a dark frame, or nothing at all.

The "View project" link is not a button. It's text with an arrow, zinc-400, that turns crimson on hover. Understatement.

### Skills

No skill bars. Skill bars are an insult to the viewer's intelligence.

Instead: a flex-wrap grid of tags. Each tag is zinc-900 background, white text, 0.5rem padding, 0.5rem border-radius. They sit comfortably in rows, wrapping naturally.

Categories are optional. If used, they appear as subtle dividers—just a line of micro-label text above each group.

This section says: here is what I know. I'm not ranking it for you.

### Contact

Four links maximum. Email. Phone. LinkedIn. GitHub. Each is a horizontal card:

- zinc-900 background
- 1rem 1.5rem padding
- crimson icon in a small square on the left
- Label and value stacked on the right

On hover, background shifts to zinc-800. Not a dramatic change—just an acknowledgment.

No contact form. Contact forms say "I don't trust you with my email address."

### Footer

One line. Centered. zinc-500. "© 2026 [Name] · Stockholm, Sverige"

If you want the PORTFOLYO badge: "Skapad med PORTFOLYO" with PORTFOLYO as a subtle link.

That's it. Nothing else. No social icons. No back-to-top button. The scroll bar is enough.

## THE CV

### First Glance (5 Seconds)

The recruiter's eye lands. What do they see?

Name at the top. Large but not enormous—2.5rem. Bold. Left-aligned. Below it, title in muted. Below that, a single horizontal line in the accent color—not the full width, just 2rem. A period at the end of a sentence, not a border.

To the right of the name block: contact details in a compact vertical list. Email, phone, city, LinkedIn. Small text, muted color. This is metadata, not content.

No photo unless the culture demands it. This CV assumes competence is in the content, not the face.

Below the header: whitespace. The eye rests. Then the content begins.

### Structure

Two levels of hierarchy only:

1. Section titles: all-caps, letter-spaced, accent color, 1rem. Separated by a hairline.
2. Content: normal case, readable, tight but not cramped.

Sections in order:

1. Summary (optional, 2-3 lines max)
2. Experience
3. Education
4. Skills
5. Languages (if relevant)
6. Certifications (if relevant)

No "Interests" section. If your interests matter, they'll come up in the interview.

### Experience Entries

Each entry is a single visual unit:

- Job title and company on the same line: title in bold, company in regular weight, separated by an en-dash
- Period right-aligned, same line, muted
- Below: description in 2-3 bullet points, each starting with a strong verb, each quantified if possible

Bullets are tight: 0.25rem margin between. The recruiter skims, and every line must deliver.

No paragraphs of prose. Prose is for novelists. This is a document of evidence.

### Skills Section

Not a tag cloud. Not a grid of logos.

Instead: 2-3 category lines.

- "Languages:" followed by a comma-separated list
- "Frameworks:" followed by a comma-separated list
- "Tools:" followed by a comma-separated list

Each list is ranked by proficiency, strongest first. You don't label proficiency. The order speaks.

### Typography

The CV uses a single font family. Space Grotesk or Inter—geometric, clean, no serifs unless the person works in law or academia.

Font sizes:

- Name: 2.5rem
- Section titles: 1rem
- Body: 0.875rem or 10pt

Line height: 1.4 for density, 1.5 for readability. Balance based on content length.

Colors (dark variant):

- Background: #0a0a0a
- Text: #ffffff
- Muted: #666666
- Accent: crimson
- Borders: #222222

Colors (light variant):

- Background: #ffffff
- Text: #1a1a1a
- Muted: #666666
- Accent: #1a1a1a (yes, black—this is corporate)
- Borders: #e5e5e5

### What It Avoids

- Infographics
- Timelines with boxes and arrows
- QR codes
- Photos (usually)
- "References available upon request" (obviously)
- Objective statements ("Seeking a challenging role...")
- Soft skills listed as skills
- Any word appearing more than necessary

### What It Signals

- This person edits ruthlessly
- This person respects my time
- This person has taste
- This person will not clutter my Slack with noise

## THE DNA, ABSTRACTED

Both the portfolio and CV share:

1. **Hierarchy by absence**: What you remove creates the hierarchy. The portfolio's first screen is 90% empty. The CV's header has one decorative element. Everything else is content or nothing.

2. **Crimson as punctuation**: The accent color appears sparingly—a dot, a line, a border. It never fills a surface. It marks moments of importance like a highlighter held by someone who's read the text three times.

3. **Proportion over decoration**: A 7rem name needs 5rem of breathing room below it. A 2-column grid needs 1.5rem gap. These proportions are felt, not calculated. They feel right because they're neither cramped nor sparse—they're inevitable.

4. **Movement as presence**: The floating orbs, the pulsing dots, the hover lifts—these are not animations, they're signs of life. The page breathes because a static page is dead. But it breathes slowly, like someone confident enough to pause between sentences.

5. **Left-aligned as default**: Centered text is for invitations and tombstones. Professional content aligns left because that's how you read. The portfolio only centers what deserves the stage: the name on first view.

6. **Sans-serif with one exception**: Space Grotesk for body, Bebas Neue for display. The display font earns its place by being used rarely. If everything is bold, nothing is.

7. **Muted until hovered**: Secondary text lives at zinc-400 or zinc-500. It doesn't demand attention—it's there when you look. On hover, it becomes white. The interface rewards curiosity without punishing indifference.

## WHAT THIS CANNOT BE

This cannot feel:

- Like a template from a gallery
- Like Squarespace or Wix
- Like someone following a tutorial
- Like someone who used every feature

This must feel:

- Like one person made every decision
- Like that person said "no" more than "yes"
- Like confidence does not need explanation
- Like silence is a choice, not a limitation

---

If a user follows these principles and fails, the system failed.
If a user ignores these principles and succeeds, the benchmark was wrong.

Neither will happen. The DNA is sound.
